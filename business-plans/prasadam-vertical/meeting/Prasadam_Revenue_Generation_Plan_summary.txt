Prasadam Revenue Generation Plan
September 26, 2026

Overview
The meeting proposed building a scalable sweets, savories, prasadam, gifting, catering, and restaurant business using temple resources, networks, technology, and the ISKCON brand.
Business Model
- Mukunda described multiple revenue channels: e-commerce, prasadam, corporate gifting, white-label products, festival stalls, daily snacks, catering, restaurant operations, QSRs, and eventual exports.
- Target customers include schools and colleges such as MNR, companies with thousands of employees, event-management firms, chambers of commerce, BNI, Round Table, gated communities, donors, and temple visitors.
- Corporate offerings should include branded packaging, gift combos, samples, menus, pricing, and both premium and competitive product segments.
Operations
- Production would initially use the existing internal department, with Prabhu providing samples, costing, and supply capacity. The group discussed weekly repeat orders, larger festival volumes, and additional staffing when required.
- Priority products should have 15–20 days of shelf life, including chikkis, kaja, khara and sweet items, puffed-rice products, pickles, papads, powders, and namkins. Packaging options include see-through boxes, branded stickers, and wooden gift boxes.
- Festival selling should expand beyond one counter to high-traffic locations such as entrances, exits, walking paths, the goshala area, and garden spaces; permanent or rain-protected pagodas were also discussed.
Decisions And Risks
- The ISKCON name and goodwill were identified as a major customer-acquisition advantage, especially for preservative-free and satvik products.
- Revenue, effort, time, costs, staffing, and return on investment must be tracked clearly through DMTT and related systems.
- Open questions: What product list, quantities, budget, pricing model, and production capacity should be finalized? Which infrastructure and staffing investments are justified before demand is validated?

Outline
Strategic Vision
- Establish prasadam and sweets as a major organizational vertical across Hyderabad, India, and potentially international markets.
- Combine production capacity with technology, marketing, databases, AI, social media, WhatsApp, Google Ads, Meta Ads, and e-commerce.
- Use the ISKCON brand to build trust and reduce customer-acquisition barriers.
Revenue Segments
- Corporate gifting and white-label branded products.
- Temple restaurant, festival counters, daily snacks, and catering.
- Gated-community flea markets and external sales.
- QSR and restaurant expansion, including a possible 100-restaurant vision in Hyderabad.
- E-commerce sales, donor orders, events, ceremonies, and exports.
Products And Packaging
- Shelf-stable sweets, savories, chikkis, kaja, puffed-rice products, namkins, pickles, papads, and spice powders.
- Premium and competitive product tiers.
- Gift boxes with customer branding, stickers, samples, menus, and pricing.
- Packaging formats discussed included see-through plastic boxes and wooden boxes.
Supply And Capacity
- Prabhu to support samples, feasibility, pricing, production, and order fulfillment.
- Use historical order trends to estimate regular and festival demand.
- Add personnel during peak campaigns and consider exclusive production resources.
- Develop kitchen, storage, infrastructure, and potentially permanent covered stalls as demand grows.
Controls And Growth
- Track revenue, expenses, time, effort, staffing, and return on investment.
- Begin with internal demand and repeat weekly orders before expanding externally.
- Use festival campaigns such as Janmashtami, Ganesh Chaturthi, New Year, and other major temple events to validate scale.
