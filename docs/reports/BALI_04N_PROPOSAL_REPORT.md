## 3. Day-by-day itinerary with observations

### Day 1: Arrival, Kuta to Ubud (2 nights). Meals: lunch, dinner
- Arrive Bali (DPS) at 12:05, then travel to Ubud via the Monkey Forest (about 38 km, 1 h 30 min).
- Lunch at an Indian restaurant.
- Tirta Empul, the holy spring water temple.
- Sri Sri Radha Madhava Ashram, ISKCON.
- Late-evening check-in at the hotel, then dinner at an Indian restaurant.

**Observations**
- **This is the most overloaded day.** The group lands after an overnight flight and a KL connection. Immigration and baggage for 15 to 30 people on visa-on-arrival can take 1.5 to 2 hours. That leaves a very short window for four stops, and the hotel check-in is "late evening".
- Tirta Empul involves ritual bathing. The group needs spare clothes and towels, and time.
- Consider moving Tirta Empul to Day 2 and keeping Day 1 light (airport, lunch, ISKCON ashram, early check-in).

### Day 2: Ubud. Meals: breakfast, lunch, dinner
- Ulun Danu Beratan Temple with Lake Bratan.
- Lunch at an Indian restaurant.
- Ubud village tour and Saraswati Temple.
- Dinner at an Indian restaurant.

**Observations**
- Ulun Danu Beratan is at Bedugul in the highlands. It is roughly 1.5 to 2 hours' drive each way from Ubud. It is cooler there, so carry a light jacket. This makes Day 2 a long day on the road.
- The Saraswati Temple and the village tour are short and close together in central Ubud.

### Day 3: Ubud to Kuta (2 nights). Meals: breakfast, lunch, dinner
- Check out, transfer to Kuta, with a shopping stop at Krishna Oleh Oleh on the way.
- Lunch, then hotel check-in.
- Evening at Tanah Lot sea temple, with local shopping.
- Dinner at an Indian restaurant.

**Observations**
- Tanah Lot is a sunset site and is crowded. Access to the rock temple depends on the tide, and non-Hindus (and sometimes everyone) cannot enter the inner shrine.

### Day 4: Kuta. Meals: breakfast, lunch, dinner
- Garuda Wisnu Kencana (GWK) Cultural Park.
- Lunch at an Indian restaurant.
- Uluwatu Temple.
- Dinner at an Indian restaurant.

**Observations**
- Uluwatu has aggressive monkeys. Secure glasses, phones and bags.
- The evening Kecak fire dance at Uluwatu is usually a separate ticket. Check whether it is covered under "entrance fees" (see the contradiction in section 7).

### Day 5: Departure. Meals: breakfast only
- Check out, shopping at Krishna Bali (subject to time).
- Airport transfer for the 16:25 flight.

**Observations**
- No lunch is included. With a 16:25 flight, guests will need to be at the airport around 13:30, so lunch is a gap to plan for.
- The shopping stop is "subject to time", so it may be dropped.

### Slab strategy
Moving up a slab lowers the price for *everyone*, so it can be cheaper than it looks to fill a few more seats. Example (Option 1, twin, tour cost before GST):
- A group of 19 has a total of ₹16,53,000. Adding 1 guest (20 pax) raises the total by only **₹57,000**, and every guest's price drops by ₹1,500.
- Going from 18 to 20 guests adds ₹1,44,000 in total (₹72,000 per added guest).
- From 24 to 25 guests the extra cost is only **₹48,000**. From 29 to 30 it is ₹54,000.
- Groups just below a slab edge should try to recruit up to it.

### Other money points
- **Deposit and cancellation are heavy.** See section 6.
- **TCS (Tax Collected at Source)** is not mentioned in the proposal. Under Budget 2026, TCS on overseas tour packages was reported to be cut to a flat 2% (from 5% up to ₹10 lakh and 20% above). One operator's page says a 5% rate still applies if PAN is not linked to Aadhaar. At 2% it works out to roughly ₹1,700 to ₹2,100 per person on top of the GST-inclusive price. TCS is adjustable against income tax, but it is a cash outlay. Ask Veena World to confirm how it will be charged.
- **Visa and levy are excluded** (section 5).

- Search results indicate Indian passport holders can get a visa on arrival (VoA) at about **IDR 500,000** (roughly ₹2,700) for a 30-day stay, plus a **tourist levy of IDR 150,000** per person (in force since Feb 2024). ## 8. Practical tips for the group

- **Dress code:** Many Balinese temples require a sarong and sash (often provided or rented at the gate). Shoulders and knees covered. Wear easy-to-remove footwear.
- **Temple customs:** Some Bali temples ask women who are menstruating not to enter. Brief the group in advance, privately.
- **Weather:** April is the end of the wet season, so carry light rain gear. Bedugul is cool.
- **Monkeys:** Monkey Forest and Uluwatu. No loose items or food.
- **Mobility:** Several sites involve steps and uneven ground (Tanah Lot, Uluwatu, GWK). Senior devotees should be briefed.
- **Money:** Carry some cash for shopping, small temple donations and tips beyond the included ones.
- **Documents:** Passport copies, insurance papers, return tickets and hotel vouchers in hand baggage.
